Why parallel rays?
Because \(L \gg d\), the two rays from the slits to any screen point are nearly parallel. The angle each makes with the axis is essentially the same \(\theta\). This lets us form a right triangle at the slits where the extra path is the opposite side: \(\delta = d\sin\theta\).

Conditions
Bright (maxima): \(d\sin\theta = m\lambda\)
Dark (minima): \(d\sin\theta = \left(m + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\lambda\)

Screen position: \(y = L\tan\theta\)
Small angle: \(y \approx L\theta \approx m\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{d}\right)\!L\)

Derivation
\(I = CA^2\) where \(A = 2a\cos\!\left(\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}\right)\)

Phase difference:
\(\displaystyle\Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi\,\Delta r}{\lambda} = \frac{2\pi\,d\sin\theta}{\lambda}\)

Small angle \(\left(\sin\theta \approx \frac{y}{L}\right)\):
\(\displaystyle\frac{\Delta\phi}{2} = \frac{\pi\,d\,y}{\lambda\,L}\)

Define \(\displaystyle\alpha = \frac{\pi\,d}{\lambda\,L}\) so \(\displaystyle\frac{\Delta\phi}{2} = \alpha\,y\)

\(\boxed{I = I_0\cos^2(\alpha\,y)}\)
